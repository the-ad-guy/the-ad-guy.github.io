04 / Understanding the customer journey

Understand the journey.
Find what needs attention.

I would start by learning how the current marketing and website experience works. These are the areas I’d examine, drawing on my experience improving acquisition, conversion, and lead quality.

Measurement / 01 of 05

Start with a reliable picture of performance.

I would review the existing website tracking, conversion definitions, attribution setup, and channel reports. The first step is understanding what the team already measures and where the data can be trusted.

What I would look for

Missing or duplicate conversions, lost campaign parameters, disconnected CRM records, and inconsistent reporting definitions.

Experience I bring

I’ve diagnosed tracking issues, built custom CRM integrations, and replaced manual reporting with automated SQL workflows.

Useful outcome

An agreed baseline and a prioritized list of measurement gaps.

Journey analysis / 02 of 05

Find where prospective customers drop off.

I would compare how visitors from different channels move through the site, review the steps before conversion, and look for pages or forms where the journey breaks down.

What I would look for

Ad-to-page message mismatch, unnecessary navigation, unclear next steps, form friction, and differences across devices or audience segments.

Experience I bring

I’ve rebuilt landing pages for software and manufacturing clients, improving conversion quality and connecting those changes to pipeline and revenue.

Useful outcome

A short list of conversion barriers worth investigating or testing.

Prospect quality / 03 of 05

Understand which actions signal a stronger prospect.

I would examine the website actions the team can observe and compare them with lead quality and CRM outcomes. Intent signals should be supported by evidence from the existing journey.

What I would look for

Whether repeat visits, content engagement, key page views, or conversion paths distinguish prospects who later become qualified opportunities.

Experience I bring

I’ve used audience segmentation, gated research, and CRM feedback to separate broad engagement from commercially useful demand.

Useful outcome

A practical map of observable intent signals, with clear limits on what the data shows.

Conversion improvement / 04 of 05

Help prospects understand the value and the next step.

Once a friction point is understood, I would work with the team on a focused landing-page or messaging test. The change should answer a specific question about what is preventing conversion.

What I would look for

Missing benefits, weak message match, unanswered buyer questions, unclear calls to action, and avoidable steps between interest and inquiry.

Experience I bring

I’ve directed industry-specific page experiences and benefit-led creative, then measured their effect on qualified leads and purchases.

Useful outcome

A focused test with a clear metric and a decision on what to keep or refine.

Lead progression / 05 of 05

Understand what happens after the first conversion.

I would examine current lead nurture sequences with Marketing and Sales, including the messages prospects receive, when they receive them, and how follow-up connects to their earlier activity.

What I would look for

Gaps in follow-up, repeated or irrelevant messaging, unclear handoffs, and content that could help a prospect take the next step.

Experience I bring

I’ve coordinated email nurture and remarketing across long enterprise buying cycles and connected campaign evaluation to CRM outcomes.

Useful outcome

Recommendations for nurture and handoffs, supported by baseline reporting.

05 / The first 90 days

Learn the business.
Build a strong foundation.

A starting outline based on my experience: audit the current setup, investigate conversion friction, and improve nurture and reporting. I’d refine priorities with the team as I learn the business.

Month one / Days 01–30

Audit the current
marketing and analytics setup.

Understand what’s already working, how performance is measured, and where reporting or attribution needs attention.

  1. Review analytics and conversion tracking.Check website events, conversion definitions, tagging, and data quality.
  2. Examine attribution pipelines.Follow campaign data into the CRM and review how lead stages, pipeline, and revenue are reported.
  3. Assess channel performance.Review paid media, landing pages, audiences, and existing reports with the people managing them.
Working output

An audit of the current setup, baseline performance, and a prioritized list of measurement gaps.

Month two / Days 31–60

Investigate friction
and test improvements.

Use the initial audit to focus on the pages, messages, and user actions that matter most to conversion.

  1. Identify drop-off points.Compare user paths by channel, audience, and device to find where engagement fails to become an inquiry or other meaningful action.
  2. Test landing-page improvements.Work with the team on focused changes to message match, content, calls to action, or forms.
  3. Map visitor intent signals.Review which observable actions are associated with qualified prospects and which need better tracking.
Working output

A clear view of the main conversion barriers, initial test results, and a practical map of prospect intent signals.

Month three / Days 61–90

Refine follow-up.
Make performance clear.

Bring the early findings into lead nurture and establish reporting leadership can use to evaluate progress.

  1. Review and refine lead nurture.Examine sequence content, timing, and relevance with Marketing and Sales, then address gaps in follow-up.
  2. Establish baseline dashboards.Bring channel spend, conversions, lead quality, and CRM pipeline into a consistent leadership view using the available data.
  3. Recommend the next priorities.Summarize what the audit and tests revealed, what needs further investigation, and where the team should focus next.
Working output

Clearer nurture workflows, reliable baseline dashboards, and a practical set of next-step recommendations.
