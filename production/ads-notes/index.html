Chrome extension

Ads Notes

A shared change log for paid media teams. Ads Notes writes every campaign change you make into a Google Sheet you choose, so the account history lives somewhere your whole team can read, filter, and report from.

Version 3, described on this page, is in development. The build currently in the Chrome Web Store captures Google Ads notes using the older Google Apps Script setup.

Why it exists

Nobody remembers what changed three months ago.

Performance moves and the first question is always the same: what did we change, and when? Google Ads has a notes feature that is hard to export and easy to miss. Most other ad platforms have nothing at all. Ads Notes gives a team one durable record across every platform they run.

What it does

Three ways a change gets recorded.

01

Captures Google Ads notes

When you save a note inside Google Ads, Ads Notes picks it up automatically and writes a matching row to your sheet, along with the account, campaign, ad group, date, and the email of the person who logged it. You keep using the Google Ads interface exactly as you do now.

02

Manual notes on any platform

Microsoft Ads, Meta Ads, LinkedIn Ads, Reddit Ads, and the rest have no notes feature. Open the extension on any of them and log a note by hand. Ads Notes recognizes the platform you are on and fills that field in for you, defaults the date to today, and lets you backdate when you are catching up.

03

Writes to your own sheet

Notes go straight from your browser into the Google Sheet you nominate. There is no middleman server, no account to create, and no copy of your data kept anywhere else. Point several teammates at the same sheet and you get one shared history of the account.

Setup

Three steps, about a minute.

  1. Install Ads Notes from the Chrome Web Store.
  2. Open the extension, click Connect, and sign in with the Google account that can edit your sheet.
  3. Paste the URL of the Google Sheet you want notes written to. Ads Notes checks it can reach the sheet and offers to add the column headers if they are not there yet.

Version 3 replaces the old setup, which asked you to deploy a Google Apps Script and copy a web app URL and a shared secret into the extension.

What it accesses

The least access that makes it work.

Your chosen spreadsheet

Ads Notes requests permission to see and edit Google Sheets, then uses it only on the spreadsheet whose URL you paste. It reads the header row to work out which column is which, and appends note rows. It does not browse your Drive or open other files.

Your email address

Used to fill the User column so a team sharing one sheet can tell who logged which note. It is written to your sheet and nowhere else.

Campaign details on the page

On an ad platform tab, Ads Notes reads the account, campaign, and ad group names shown on screen so it can label the note. This happens in your browser and only on advertising platform pages.
